2. Nature and Limitations of Calculations

Our calculators use well-established, medically recognized formulas (such as the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for BMR, and World Health Organization standards for BMI). However, these formulas are general estimates and cannot fully account for each individual's unique body composition, metabolism, genetic factors, hormonal status, or medical history.

For example, BMI is based only on height and weight and does not account for muscle mass, body composition, or fat distribution. A highly muscular athlete may show as "overweight" by BMI due to muscle weight, even though their body fat percentage is quite low. Similarly, calorie needs calculations are also estimates — actual needs may be higher or lower based on various factors.

  • Individual metabolic rate and genetic factors

For this reason, we do not guarantee any specific amount of weight loss or gain, nor any specific timeline. Results vary considerably from person to person, and some people may need significantly more time than others to see meaningful, lasting results.
